# Score scale and exposure

A display rating summarizes a declared event estimand. It does not directly measure talent, psychological intent, off-ball execution or causal match impact. The implemented [mapping](../../matchpulse/ratings.py) anchors at 6, divides contribution by the square root of (minutes + 30) / 90, then applies `2 tanh(2.5 x)`. That saturates toward 4 and 8 even though the display contract permits 0 to 10. Extreme endpoints therefore are not reachable with this baseline. The interval is clipped to the declared display bounds, but is not a calibrated posterior interval.

Thirty minutes of prior exposure limits early instability. It does not create evidence for a substitute who never appears. A player needs positive observed minutes before receiving a rating. Short appearances, untrusted feed support and unfinished or interrupted fixtures remain provisional. The [exposure tests](../../tests/test_ratings.py) independently check an unused substitute and a ten-minute appearance. The authored dismissal occurs at 2,000 seconds; subsequent events cannot increase that player's minutes.

A useful production scale would require repeatability, role-specific calibration, response to missing fields and agreement with independent evidence. It should disclose how much ranking variance comes from the display transformation. Those studies have not run on actual football data. Printing two decimals improves deterministic revision comparison, but supplies no evidence that 7.63 is empirically distinguishable from 7.58. The UI always displays the support interval and baseline status beside the number.
